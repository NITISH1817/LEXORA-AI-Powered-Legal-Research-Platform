### Dossier 1: `bench_093` — Article 21 Evolution & Inter-Judicial Distinctions
- **Query ID:** `bench_093`
- **Benchmark Split:** `dev` | **Category:** `MULTI_PASSAGE_SYNTHESIS`

- **Legal Subject:** Constitutional Law — Article 21, Article 14, Article 19 ("Golden Triangle")
- **Bench / Jurisdiction:** Supreme Court of India

#### Exact Legal Question:
> *Trace the evolution of Article 21 from literal procedure in A.K. Gopalan to just, fair, and reasonable procedure in Maneka Gandhi and privacy in Puttaswamy.*

#### Verified Source Identifiers & Citations:
1. *A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras*: AIR 1950 SC 27; 1950 SCR 88 (6-Judge Bench, decided 19-05-1950).
2. *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India*: (1978) 1 SCC 248; (1978) 2 SCR 621 (7-Judge Bench, decided 25-01-1978).
3. *Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India*: (2017) 10 SCC 1; (2017) 10 SCR 1 (9-Judge Constitution Bench, decided 24-08-2017).

#### Primary Source Excerpts & Paragraph References:
- **Maneka Gandhi** (1978) 1 SCC 248, per P.N. Bhagwati, J. (Paras 56–58):
  > "The principle of reasonableness, which legally as well as philosophically, is an essential element of equality or non-arbitrariness pervades Article 14 like a brooding omnipresence and the procedure contemplated by Article 21 must answer the test of reasonableness in order to be in conformity with Article 14. It must be 'right and just and fair' and not arbitrary, fanciful or oppressive; otherwise, it would be no procedure at all..."
- **Puttaswamy** (2017) 10 SCC 1, per Dr. D.Y. Chandrachud, J. (plurality, Paras 294–298):
  > "Privacy is the constitutional core of human dignity. Article 21 has received an expansive interpretation from Gopalan to Maneka Gandhi and beyond. Gopalan's isolationist view of fundamental rights was discarded in R.C. Cooper and Maneka Gandhi... Privacy is an intrinsic part of the right to life and personal liberty under Article 21."

#### Procedural Posture & Statutory Versions:
- *Gopalan:* Writ petition under Article 32 challenging Section 14 of Preventive Detention Act, 1950.
- *Maneka Gandhi:* Writ petition under Article 32 challenging impounding of passport under Section 10(3)(c) of Passports Act, 1967.
- *Puttaswamy:* Reference by a 3-judge bench arising from writ petitions challenging the biometric Aadhaar scheme.

#### Proposed Expected Answer Points:
1. *A.K. Gopalan* adopted a literal, positivist construction: "procedure established by law" meant any valid enacted law; rejected American "due process of law"; held Articles 19 and 21 were mutually exclusive watertight compartments.
2. *Maneka Gandhi* rejected the watertight compartment theory, establishing the "Golden Triangle" of Articles 14, 19, and 21. Procedure depriving personal liberty must be "right, just, and fair," not arbitrary, fanciful, or oppressive.
3. *Puttaswamy* reaffirmed that personal liberty under Article 21 encompasses informational, bodily, and decisional privacy as intrinsic to human dignity, protected by a strict 3-fold proportionality standard.

#### Majority, Concurring, and Dissenting Considerations:
- In *Maneka Gandhi*, Bhagwati, J. wrote for plurality; Krishna Iyer, J. and Beg, C.J. delivered influential concurrences emphasizing procedural natural justice.
- In *Puttaswamy*, six separate opinions were authored; reviewers must verify the unanimous 9-0 joint signed order is cited as the binding holding.

#### Subsequent Treatment & Overruling Issues:
- *Gopalan* was overruled on its mutually exclusive fundamental rights doctrine by *R.C. Cooper v. Union of India* (1970) and *Maneka Gandhi* (1978).

#### Explicit Questions for Advocate Judgment:
1. Does the proposed answer point accurately characterize the Indian doctrine as procedural fairness with non-arbitrariness rather than verbatim American substantive due process?
2. Are the paragraph citations to Bhagwati, J. (Paras 56–58) and Chandrachud, J. (Paras 294–298) sufficient and accurate?

#### Primary Source Excerpts & Paragraph References:
- **Kharak Singh** (1964) 1 SCR 332, per N. Rajagopala Ayyangar, J. (for 4:2 majority at p. 349):
  > "The right of privacy is not a guaranteed right under our Constitution and therefore the attempt to ascertain the movements of an individual which is merely a manner in which privacy is invaded is not an infringement of a fundamental right."
- **Kharak Singh**, per K. Subba Rao, J. (dissenting for himself and Shah, J., at p. 359):
  > "The right to personal liberty in Article 21 implies a right to be free from restrictions or encroachments on one's privacy...   > "The decision in Kharak Singh to the extent that it reads that the right to privacy is not a fundamental right is expressly overruled."

#### Procedural Posture & Statutory Versions:
- *Kharak Singh:* Article 32 petition challenging Chapter XX of U.P. Police Regulations (Reg. 236 authorizing secret picketing, domiciliary visits at night, and tracking).
- *Puttaswamy:* Reference by 3-judge bench resolving whether privacy is a fundamental right.

#### Proposed Expected Answer Points:
1. In *Kharak Singh*, the majority struck down Regulation 236(b) (domiciliary night visits) as violating Article 21, but upheld shadow surveillance and held the Indian Constitution does not guarantee a general right to privacy. Subba Rao, J. dissented, holding personal liberty encompasses privacy.
2. In *Puttaswamy*, the 9-judge bench **partially overruled** *Kharak Singh* specifically to the extent it held privacy was not a fundamental right, while **affirming** Subba Rao, J.'s minority opinion and upholding the invalidation of domiciliary night visits.

#### Explicit Questions for Advocate Judgment:
1. Does the answer explicitly avoid claiming that *Kharak Singh* was overruled in its entirety? (Crucial test: domiciliary visit strike-down was approved).

### Dossier 4: `bench_121` — IBC Section 53 Waterfall Priority vs Statutory Tax Dues

- **Source Verification:** `SOURCE_VERIFICATION_PENDING` (Official SCC judgment check required)
- **Legal Subject:** Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC) — Distribution Waterfall
- **Bench / Jurisdiction:** Supreme Court of India

#### Exact Legal Question:
> *Under Section 53 of the IBC, where do government taxes rank relative to unsecured financial creditors?*

#### Verified Source Identifiers & Citations:
1. Section 53(1), Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (Act 31 of 2016).
2. *State Tax Officer v. Rainbow Papers Ltd.*: (2023) 9 SCC 545 (2-Judge Bench, decided 06-09-2022).
3. *Paschimanchal Vidyut Vitran Nigam Ltd. v. Raman Ispat Pvt. Ltd.*: (2023) 10 SCC 60 (3-Judge Bench, decided 17-07-2023).

#### Controlling Statutory Text (IBC Section 53(1) Distribution Waterfall):
- (a) Insolvency resolution process costs and liquidation costs.
- (b) Pari passu: (i) Workmen's dues for 24 months, and (ii) Debts owed to secured creditors who relinquished security.
- (c) Wages and unpaid dues to other employees for 12 months.
- (d) Financial debts owed to **unsecured creditors**.
- (e) Pari passu: (i) Any amount due to the **Central Government and State Government** (taxes/duties for 2 years), and (ii) Debts owed to secured creditors for unpaid balance after enforcement.
- (f) Any remaining debts and dues.

#### Conflict & Harmonization Analysis:
- In *Rainbow Papers* (2022), a 2-judge bench held that Section 48 of the Gujarat VAT Act created a statutory first charge making the State a "secured creditor" under Section 3(30) of IBC.
- In *Paschimanchal Vidyut* (2023), a larger 3-judge bench distinguished and confined *Rainbow Papers*, holding that Section 53(1)(e) explicitly categorizes government statutory dues, placing them **strictly subordinate** to unsecured financial creditors under Section 53(1)(d). The non-obstante clause in Section 238 overrides state statutory first charges.

#### Proposed Expected Answer Points:
1. Under Section 53(1) of the IBC, government taxes and statutory dues rank under **Clause (e)**.
2. Unsecured financial creditors rank under **Clause (d)**.
3. Therefore, unsecured financial debts have **clear statutory priority** over government taxes.
4. *Paschimanchal Vidyut* (2023) confirmed this priority and confined *Rainbow Papers*, ruling that statutory first charges cannot elevate government tax claims over the Section 53 waterfall.

#### Explicit Questions for Advocate Judgment:
1. Does the answer properly articulate the distinction between contractual security interests and statutory charges?
2. Does it accurately state that Clause (d) ranks ahead of Clause (e)?

#### Verified Source Identifiers & Citations:

2. *Associate Builders v. DDA*: (2015) 3 SCC 49 (2-Judge Bench, decided 25-11-2014).
3. *Ssangyong Engineering & Construction Co. Ltd. v. NHAI*: (2019) 15 SCC 131 (2-Judge Bench, decided 08-05-2019).

#### Controlling Statutory Text & Amendments:
- The Arbitration and Conciliation (Amendment) Act, 2015 inserted Section 34(2A):
  > "An arbitral award arising out of an arbitrations other than international commercial arbitrations, may also be set aside by the Court, if the Court finds that the award is vitiated by patent illegality appearing on the face of the award:

#### Proposed Expected Answer Points:
1. Pre-2015, *Associate Builders* treated patent illegality as an expansive facet of public policy under Section 34(2)(b)(ii).
2. The 2015 Amendment carved patent illegality out into a separate, independent statutory ground under **Section 34(2A)**.
3. **Domestic Arbitrations Only:** Patent illegality is restricted strictly to domestic arbitrations seated in India; it is unavailable for international commercial arbitrations seated in India or foreign seated awards.
4. **Proviso Limitations:** Merely erroneous application of law or reappreciation of evidence is expressly prohibited as a ground for patent illegality.
5. In *Ssangyong Engineering* (2019), the Supreme Court ruled that patent illegality must go to the root of the matter and cannot entail reviewing the merits or replacing the arbitrator's plausible contractual interpretation.

#### Explicit Questions for Advocate Judgment:
1. Does the candidate answer highlight that patent illegality is excluded from international commercial arbitrations?
2. Does it accurately state the statutory prohibition against reappreciating evidence?

- **Query ID:** `bench_072`
- **Benchmark Split:** `dev` | **Category:** `OVERRULED_PRECEDENT`
- **Risk Score:** `7.0` | **Priority:** `PRIORITY_1_CRITICAL`
- **Lifecycle State:** `PENDING_HUMAN_LEGAL_REVIEW` (**BLOCKED**)
- **Source Verification:** `SOURCE_VERIFICATION_PENDING` (Official 2024 SCC / INSC check required)
- **Legal Subject:** Commercial Arbitration & Stamp Duty Law
- **Bench / Jurisdiction:** Supreme Court of India

#### Exact Legal Question:
> *What is the current status of the law regarding unstamped arbitration agreements following the 7-judge Constitution Bench ruling?*

#### Verified Source Identifiers & Citations:
1. *N.N. Global Mercantile Pvt. Ltd. v. Indo Unique Flame Ltd.*: (2023) 7 SCC 1 (5-Judge Bench, decided 25-04-2023).
2. *In Re: Interplay Between Arbitration Agreements under Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 and Indian Stamp Act 1899*: (2024) 6 SCC 1; 2023 INSC 1066 (7-Judge Constitution Bench, decided 13-12-2023).

#### Doctrinal Evolution & Ratios:
- In *NN Global (2023)*, a 3:2 majority held that an unstamped or deficiently stamped underlying commercial contract is void and non-existent under Section 2(g) of the Contract Act and Section 35 of the Stamp Act, meaning no valid arbitration clause exists for Section 8 or 11 referral.
- In *In Re Interplay (2024)*, a unanimous 7-judge Constitution Bench **expressly overruled** *NN Global (2023)* and held:
  1. Non-stamping is a **curable defect** under the Stamp Act, not an incurable invalidity making the contract void ab initio.
  2. Doctrine of Separability (Section 16): An arbitration agreement is legally distinct and independent from the underlying contract.
  3. Competence-Competence: Courts at Section 8 and Section 11 stage examine only the prima facie existence of the arbitration agreement, leaving impounding and stamp duty objections to be resolved by the arbitral tribunal.

#### Proposed Expected Answer Points:
1. Current law is governed by the 7-judge Constitution Bench in *In Re Interplay* (2024), which unanimously overruled the 5-judge bench in *NN Global* (2023).
2. Unstamped or insufficiently stamped arbitration agreements are **not void ab initio**; non-stamping is a curable defect under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899.
3. Referral courts under Section 8 or Section 11 of the Arbitration Act cannot impound the document or reject appointment; they must refer the parties to arbitration, deferring stamp duty adjudication to the arbitral tribunal under Section 16.

#### Explicit Questions for Advocate Judgment:
1. Does the answer distinguish between "inadmissibility in evidence" (Section 35 Stamp Act) and "voidness in law" (Section 2(g) Contract Act)?
2. Does it cite *In Re Interplay* as the controlling, binding 7-judge precedent?

### Dossier 7: `bench_056` — The 3-Fold Proportionality Standard for Privacy Restrictions
- **Query ID:** `bench_056`
- **Benchmark Split:** `dev` | **Category:** `CONSTITUTIONAL_STANDARD`
- **Risk Score:** `6.8` | **Priority:** `PRIORITY_1_CRITICAL`
- **Lifecycle State:** `PENDING_HUMAN_LEGAL_REVIEW` (**BLOCKED**)
- **Source Verification:** `SOURCE_VERIFICATION_PENDING` (Official 2017 SCC Volume check required)
- **Legal Subject:** Constitutional Law — Limitations on Fundamental Rights
- **Bench / Jurisdiction:** Supreme Court of India

#### Exact Legal Question:
> *What is the 3-fold proportionality standard laid down in Puttaswamy for testing state encroachment on privacy?*

#### Verified Source Identifiers & Citations:
- *Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India*: (2017) 10 SCC 1; (2017) 10 SCR 1, per Dr. D.Y. Chandrachud, J. (at Para 310) and Sanjay Kishan Kaul, J. (at Paras 638–642).

#### Controlling Legal Standard (Cumulative 3-Prong Test):
Any state interference with the fundamental right to privacy must satisfy three cumulative conditions:
1. **Legality:** The action must be sanctioned by a valid, enacted parliamentary or legislative law (executive action, police manuals, or circulars alone cannot abridge privacy).
2. **Legitimate State Aim:** The law must serve a legitimate public goal that falls within the constitutional zone of state interests (e.g., national security, crime prevention, public order).
3. **Proportionality:** The means adopted by the legislature must be proportional to the object sought to be achieved:
   - There must be a rational nexus between the object and the measure.
   - The measure must be the least intrusive means (necessity).
   - The state action must not have a disproportionate impact on the individual (strict proportionality).

#### Proposed Expected Answer Points:
1. The 3-fold proportionality standard in *Puttaswamy* requires: (i) Legality, (ii) Legitimate State Aim, and (iii) Proportionality in means.
2. The requirements are **cumulative**: failure of any single prong renders the state action unconstitutional under Article 21.
3. Procedural safeguards against abuse and independent oversight are required elements of the proportionality prong.

#### Explicit Questions for Advocate Judgment:
1. Does the expected answer state that the three prongs are cumulative?
2. Does it make clear that mere executive fiat without statutory backing fails on the legality prong alone?
