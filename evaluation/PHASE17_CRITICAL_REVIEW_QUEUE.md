#### Procedural Posture & Key Dates:
1. *A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras*: Decided May 19, 1950 by 6-Judge Bench (AIR 1950 SC 27 / (1950) SCR 88). Writ petition under Article 32 challenging preventive detention under Section 14 of Preventive Detention Act, 1950.
2. *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India*: Decided Jan 25, 1978 by 7-Judge Bench ((1978) 1 SCC 248 / (1978) 2 SCR 621). Writ petition under Article 32 challenging impounding of passport under Section 10(3)(c) of Passports Act, 1967 without furnishing reasons.
3. *Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India*: Decided Aug 24, 2017 by 9-Judge Constitution Bench ((2017) 10 SCC 1 / (2017) 10 SCR 1). Reference arising out of challenges to Aadhaar scheme concerning constitutional status of right to privacy.

#### Relevant Judgment Excerpt & Paragraph References:
- *Maneka Gandhi* (1978) 1 SCC 248, per Bhagwati, J. (at Paras 56–58):
  > "The principle of reasonableness, which legally as well as philosophically, is an essential element of equality or non-arbitrariness pervades Article 14 like a brooding omnipresence and the procedure contemplated by Article 21 must answer the test of reasonableness in order to be in conformity with Article 14. It must be 'right and just and fair' and not arbitrary, fanciful or oppressive; otherwise, it would be no procedure at all and the requirement of Article 21 would not be satisfied."
- *Puttaswamy* (2017) 10 SCC 1, per Dr. D.Y. Chandrachud, J. (for plurality at Paras 294–298):
  > "Privacy is the constitutional core of human dignity. Article 21 has received an expansive interpretation from Gopalan to Maneka Gandhi and beyond. Gopalan's isolationist view of fundamental rights was discarded in R.C. Cooper and Maneka Gandhi... Privacy is an intrinsic part of the right to life and personal liberty under Article 21."

#### Proposed Answer Points & Supporting Authorities:
1. **Gopalan (1950):** Literal textualist interpretation. 'Procedure established by law' meant state-enacted legislative procedure; rejected American 'due process of law'. Held fundamental rights operate in mutually exclusive, watertight compartments. (Authority: (1950) SCR 88).
2. **Maneka Gandhi (1978):** Expressly overruled Gopalan's watertight compartment theory. Held Articles 14, 19, and 21 form an interconnected 'golden triangle'. Procedure deprivation must be 'just, fair, and reasonable' and non-arbitrary. (Authority: (1978) 1 SCC 248).
3. **Puttaswamy (2017):** Unanimously held privacy is an intrinsic fundamental right under Article 21. Elevated protection via 3-fold proportionality test (legality, legitimate state goal, proportionality). (Authority: (2017) 10 SCC 1).

#### Majority vs Concurring vs Dissenting Nuances:
- *Puttaswamy* delivered six separate opinions with a unanimous joint order. Reviewers must verify candidate answers cite the unanimous ratio, not isolated obiter from individual concurring opinions (e.g. Chelameswar, J. on informational privacy vs Bobde, J. on natural rights).

#### Overruled / Distinguishing Authorities:
- *A.K. Gopalan* literal compartment doctrine expressly overruled in *R.C. Cooper* (1970) 1 SCC 248 and *Maneka Gandhi* (1978) 1 SCC 248.

#### Specific Questions for Advocate Judgment:
